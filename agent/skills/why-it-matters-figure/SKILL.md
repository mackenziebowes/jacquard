If the piece has no "what happened" figure for the idea yet (check the page and its figure registry), load `explainer-figure` and make that first.

# Why it matters

The first figure helped the reader parse what happened. This one helps them parse what follows from it. Same reader: someone who struggles with words and charts. Same tools: a physical scene, a small puppet show, numbers as labels and never as axes. Follow the Drawing and Done rules from `explainer-figure`.

## Before drawing

1. Start from the first figure's claim. Ask: so what? Write down what follows from it, in one sentence, using what the piece itself says or clearly implies. Don't invent stakes the author didn't raise.
2. Name who it lands on: a reader, a customer, a builder, the author. Put them in the scene. The first figure shows the machine; this one shows a person next to it and what happens to them, or what they can now do.
3. Name how the reader should feel about it, at the author's strength: wary, relieved, curious, invited. Draw that through the scene (who is waiting, who is left out, where the opportunity is), not with captions that say it. The figure must not be louder than the author.
4. Reuse the first figure's metaphor where you can, so the two read as one story told twice: first the event, then its consequence.

## Placing it

Put it where the piece turns from what happened to what it means, usually after the first figure, often a few paragraphs later. If the piece never makes that turn, say so and don't make the figure.
